Human Trafficking Education

Know what trafficking is, recognize what it may look like, understand why leaving can be difficult, and learn how to respond.

Start Here

What is human trafficking?

Human trafficking involves the use of force, fraud, or coercion to obtain labor or a commercial sex act.

Trafficking is not limited to one neighborhood, one type of victim, or one kind of trafficker. It can happen in familiar settings and can involve complex relationships, financial dependence, threats, isolation, manipulation, abuse, or control.

Where Exploitation Can Occur

Look beyond stereotypes.

Homes & Relationships

Trafficking and exploitation can be connected to intimate partner violence, family obligation, isolation, threats, withheld identification, and financial dependence.

Hospitality & Services

Hotels, motels, restaurants, beauty and massage businesses, health care services, and domestic work may be settings where warning signs become visible.

Work & Labor

Agriculture, construction, traveling sales crews, seasonal work, temporary labor, and other industries can involve labor exploitation and trafficking.

Possible Indicators

Warning signs are context, not proof.

No single sign confirms trafficking. Look for patterns of control, fear, exploitation, restricted freedom, or circumstances that do not add up.

Control

  • Communication appears controlled by someone else.
  • Someone has limited access to identification or personal documents.
  • Another person speaks for them or monitors interactions.
  • They seem unable to leave a work or living situation freely.

Behavior & Safety

  • Signs of abuse or branding.
  • Avoiding eye contact or showing fear around a controlling person.
  • Distrust of authority or reluctance to answer basic questions.
  • Hopelessness, dissociation, or denial that exploitation is occurring.

Youth Vulnerabilities

  • Isolation or serious problems within the home.
  • Prior sexual abuse or involvement in foster care / juvenile systems.
  • Online grooming and social media manipulation.
  • Gang involvement or relationships used to create dependency.
Why Someone May Stay

Control can be powerful and complicated.

A victim or survivor may not identify as a victim, may believe the situation is their choice, may feel loyalty to the person exploiting them, or may believe there is no safe alternative.

  • Trauma bonding
  • Threats to family or loved ones
  • Social isolation
  • Financial dependency
  • Withholding identification or documents
  • Abuse, fear, and coercion
Common Misconceptions

What trafficking does not always look like.

“They were kidnapped.”

Kidnapping can happen, but many trafficking situations develop through manipulation, fraud, coercion, abusive relationships, dependency, or exploitation of vulnerability.

“They would just leave.”

Leaving can be dangerous and complicated. Control may involve threats, housing, food, family, trauma bonds, money, documents, fear, or emotional dependency.

“It only happens to immigrants.”

Trafficking can affect citizens and non-citizens, adults and minors, and people across many backgrounds. Immigration status alone does not define trafficking.

Are smuggling and trafficking the same thing?

No. Trafficking centers on exploitation through force, fraud, or coercion. Smuggling is a separate issue involving movement across a border or boundary.

Does trafficking always involve physical restraint?

No. Control may be psychological, financial, relational, legal, or based on threats, fraud, abuse, isolation, or dependence.

If something feels wrong, learn the safest next step.
